    How to read this illustration

    Use an actor’s menu for durable or plain writes. Durable: upload to the bucket, then answer. Plain: answer, then upload; a crash can lose that write. The upload window and packet hops are stretched to make the order visible.

    Sleep illustrates 30 seconds idle (6 seconds at the default 5× speed). Other actors stay put until you act. A sleeping actor wakes where the request lands, first to claim, unless an existing hold (such as open WebSockets) names an owner. A move is not a pin. Remove drains and stops a live node; drain alone keeps it forwarding. This demo has a limit of five nodes and 30 actors.

    During takeover, eligible bodyless GET/HEAD requests and WebSocket upgrades can wait (25 s default, with caps). Writes and the first requests that discover the failure get 503, Retry-After: 1. A 503 does not prove a write never ran. Nothing is automatically retried here. 0.4.2 request rules.

    Planned, not simulated: export and backup (0.5.0); rebalancing at wake-up (0.6.0).

    How this illustration relates to crash tests

    WasmKeep 0.4.2 soak: three VPS next to the bucket, 2 hours, 39 kills, 1,264 takeovers. Writable again: 17.4 s median · 22.5 s p99 (29.0 s worst). Zero acknowledged durable writes lost; one plain write lost out of 202,535. How we measured.

    This is an illustration, not a replay or performance prediction: a schematic 10 s lease and 5 s grace run at 5× speed by default. After expiry, this illustration spreads recovery claims across live nodes and restores their actors. In a real cluster, requests and alarms trigger those claims; this is not automatic rebalancing. Packet, upload and hand-over durations are illustrative and deliberately slowed for readability.
