What would it cost?

At small and medium scale, Cloudflare Durable Objects is likely as cheap as three servers and a bucket, or cheaper. Self-hosting starts to pay with many row writes, a lot of stored data, or actors that cannot hibernate, and only on a bucket that does not bill per request. Put in your own numbers.

Every price was read on the vendor's own page on 2026-11-14, before tax, and is linked below. Euros and dollars are never added together or drawn on one axis: we do not convert currencies.

What the bill leaves out

The servers' price is the smaller part of running them.

  • Your time. Patching, monitoring, certificates, backups of the bucket, on-call, and upgrades of pre-alpha software.
  • No edge network. A cluster runs in one region; no global routing, DDoS protection or WAF in front of it unless you add them.
  • Failover takes seconds. A dead node's actors are writable again after 17.6 s at the median and 27.1 s at worst in the 0.4.1 release candidate's 2-hour soak, and after 33.7 s at worst in the 12-hour soak.
  • The bucket is a hard dependency. A node cut off from it for 8 s stops serving its actors.
  • No SLA, no compliance claims. Nobody answers for your uptime but you.
  • Egress and taxes. Not in either bill. R2 and OVHcloud do not charge egress; the VPS include traffic as their pages state.

Assumptions

Where a number comes from, and what it is not.

  • Three small VPS can carry the workload: assumed, not measured. The busiest of our soaks wrote about 60 acknowledged durable writes a second through three OVHcloud VPS-2 (643,891 in 3 hours, 648c2d1; the 12-hour run carried 53, b849bfe), with writers that wait for each answer. That is not a capacity limit, and no run measured one: past it the chart draws the self-hosted line grey and dotted. Each actor's database also lives on the disk of the node that runs it; the calculator says when your data outgrows the VPS disks.
  • About 1.03 bucket PUTs per durable write, snapshots included, measured in a 12-hour soak (benchmarks). The calculator applies it to every write.
  • The floor at rest, per node: 0.334 PUT, 1.10 LIST and 1.33 GET a second, measured on three idle VPS for ten minutes (CSV). The node's secrets read, every 3 s, was not counted then, so the calculator uses the 1.67 GET a second actually requested; the rates follow from fixed periods in the code (bucket requests at rest). For three nodes over 30 days: about 2.6 million PUTs, 8.6 million LISTs and 13 million GETs; on R2 the PUTs and LISTs alone are about 11.2 million Class A requests, about US$46 a month before any traffic.
  • Cloudflare duration is billed at 128 MB per active object; the calculator counts each request's wall-clock time as active, and every actor that never hibernates for the whole month. Requests that run on one object at the same time share its duration, so busy objects cost less than this.
  • Cloudflare rounding: usage above each allowance is rounded up to the next billable unit, as its page says. The Durable Objects page gave January 2026 as the start of SQLite storage billing; the calculator assumes it is billed.
  • A month is 30 days. The bucket holds about your stored data; history and snapshots on top of it are not modelled. Hetzner bills each object as at least 64 KB, which the calculator does not model either.
  • OVHcloud prices are "from" prices on its international site, in US dollars; prices differ by country site, and the commitment they assume is not stated. Hetzner's CX plans showed "currently unavailable" on 2026-11-14.
