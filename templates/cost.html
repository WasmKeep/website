Not in the bill

Budget for the work of running the app, as well as the infrastructure.

  • Your time. Patching, monitoring, certificates, backups of the bucket, on-call, and upgrades of pre-alpha software.
  • No edge network. A cluster runs in one region; no global routing, DDoS protection or WAF in front of it unless you add them.
  • Failover takes seconds. A dead node's actors are writable again after 17.4 s at the median and 29.0 s at worst in the 0.4.2 release candidate's 2-hour soak, and after 33.7 s at worst in the 12-hour soak (sources and workload).
  • The bucket is a hard dependency. A node cut off from it for 8 s stops serving its actors.
  • No SLA, no compliance claims. Nobody answers for your uptime but you.
  • Network charges and taxes. Not in either bill. There is no WasmKeep service fee; you pay your provider's compute, storage, request and network charges. Included traffic and overage charges depend on the provider and plan.

Evidence and assumptions

How the calculator estimates capacity, storage requests and prices. How we measured links the workloads and raw results.

  • Fleet sizing is an estimate from soak rates, not measured capacity. The cited three-node OVHcloud VPS-2 soak acknowledged 643,891 writes in 10,804.524 s: 59.6/s across the cluster, or a derived average of 19.9/s/node (Raw test results). The 12-hour run carried about 53/s (Raw test results). Neither saturated a node. We assume linear scaling, the same rate on a CX33, and half on a 2-vCPU VPS; those other VPS types were not soaked.
  • Nodes = max(3, 1 + ceiling of the largest resource requirement). Requirements are writes/s divided by the node rate, peak resident actors divided by resident slots, sockets divided by 50,000, and stored GB divided by 80% of a node's disk. One spare node is a sizing margin, not a failover guarantee. The model assumes an operator spreads actors evenly; automatic rebalancing at wake-up is planned for 0.6.0. CPU, read throughput, event peaks, relay overhead and fan-out can require more nodes. Millions of actors and large fleets have not been measured.
  • Resident and socket budgets are limits, not load-test results. We assume an instance pool of 2,000 on 8 GB plans (as in the benchmark configuration) and 1,000 on 4 GB plans; 10% is reserved for front requests, leaving 1,800 or 900 resident actors (documented pool split). Memory use varies by guest; these settings must fit RAM and the file-descriptor budget. WebSockets have fixed caps of 1,000/actor and 50,000/node (limits). Disk sizing assumes one evenly distributed copy plus 20% headroom, and does not cover a single oversized tenant or cached copies left after movement.
  • About 1.03 bucket PUTs per durable write, snapshots included, measured in a 12-hour soak (benchmarks). The 0.4.2 model applies it to every writing event, plus a separate idle floor; this deliberately retains the observed overhead and may double-count a small amount of lease traffic. Actual request mix varies.
  • The default floor at rest: derived from 0.4.2's documented cadences, not yet re-measured. Per node in a three-node cluster with one app, no alarms and no idle expiry: ⅓ PUT, 0.1 LIST and 2⅓ GET a second. The lease is one PUT every 3 s; listings are one for nodes every 30 s, three for operator intents every 60 s and one for alarms every 60 s. Heartbeat reads plus control/changed, the deployed version, its tombstone and secrets total seven GETs every 3 s (documented cadences). For three nodes over 30 days: 2,592,000 PUTs, 777,600 LISTs and 18,144,000 GETs. At the R2 prices below, after its free allowances: US$10.66 in Class A requests plus US$2.93 in Class B, about US$13.60 at fractional rates, or US$16.74 with the model's whole-million billing rounding, before traffic or storage. For N nodes the floor is N/3 PUT/s, N × 0.1 LIST/s and N × (N + 4)/3 GET/s: each node reads every node's heartbeat. The cost of large fleets can therefore grow quadratically on R2. Alarms, control changes, nodes joining and idle expiry add requests.
  • Historical 0.4.1 measurement, not the default model: 0.334 PUT, 1.10 LIST and 1.33 GET a second per node, measured on three idle VPS for ten minutes (Raw test results). The secrets read every 3 s was not counted then; including it gave the old model 1.67 GET a second. Its three-node, 30-day estimate was about 2.6 million PUTs, 8.6 million LISTs and 13 million GETs, with about US$46 in R2 Class A requests before traffic.
  • Planned batching is a projection, disabled by default. ADR 0034 proposes one upload in flight per actor. We assume all writes are eligible plain writes, no flush barriers, and evenly paced writing events during each busy period: traffic PUTs = writes × 1.03 / max(1, busy-actor writes/s × 0.057 s). The 57 ms comes from the twelve-hour test’s median, not a guaranteed round trip or batching measurement. Payload caps, snapshots, alarms, explicit durable calls and burst timing can reduce savings. Nodes and idle requests stay unchanged. Plain replies are not durable acknowledgements; unreplicated writes may be lost. Export and online backup are also planned for 0.5.0.
  • Cloudflare duration uses 128 MB (0.128 GB, following the vendor’s billing examples) per active object. Hibernation-eligible idle time is free, even before eviction from memory. Events are assumed uniform across actors: event-count × activity time is capped at actor-count × month, then the always-active fraction is replaced with a full month, avoiding double billing those objects. Real overlapping events and hibernation eligibility change duration. WebSocket opens count as Worker and DO requests; incoming messages count 20:1 as DO requests, without another Worker invocation. Outgoing messages are not charged as requests. WebSocket hibernation and billing.
  • Cloudflare Free versus Paid. Free is modeled only if steady daily usage fits 100k Worker requests (10 ms CPU/request), 100k DO requests, 13k GB-s, 5M rows read, 100k rows written and 5 GB stored. These are daily caps, not a monthly pool; other account usage, peaks and per-object limits can force Paid. Otherwise the US$5 Paid minimum and monthly included allowances apply. No paid allowance is charged twice. Rows must include index updates and scanned rows, not only returned rows. The linked pricing pages were checked on 2026-11-15.
  • Cloudflare rounding: Durable Objects and R2 usage above each allowance is rounded up to the next billable unit; Workers request and CPU charges use fractional millions, as its worked examples do. SQLite rates are verified; the billing announcement still says a January 7, 2026 target start date. The estimate applies the published SQLite charges; an actual account invoice was not checked.
  • A month is 30 days (720 hours). OVHcloud storage uses US$0.00001111/GiB-hour, rounding stored data up to GiB; its displayed US$0.0081103/GiB-month assumes 730 hours. Hetzner storage overage uses 720 hours and 100 MB increments; its included egress is 1.08 TB over 30 days, with excess egress excluded here. The bucket holds about your stored data; history and snapshots are not modelled. Hetzner’s 64 kB minimum per object is also not modelled.
  • Purchase conditions. OVHcloud’s international VPS page confirms the US-dollar “from” prices below, but does not identify the commitment behind them; country, region and checkout terms can change the quote. Hetzner’s Germany/Finland CX prices include one €0.50/month primary IPv4; both plans are marked “currently unavailable” on 2026-11-15. The calculator is not a capacity reservation or checkout quote.
